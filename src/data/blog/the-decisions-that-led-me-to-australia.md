It's been a while since I've written anything here. My last post was back in 2022, and a lot has changed since then. I've been meaning to get back to writing but life kept getting in the way - which is ironic because the things that got in the way are exactly what I want to write about. So here we go. This one's a bit more personal than my usual tech posts, but I think it's a story worth sharing.

## How it all started

When COVID hit, it changed a lot of things for a lot of people. For me, it actually opened up a door I didn't expect. I was working for a company based in Singapore, and after my daughter was born, I realised I couldn't visit my family in Sri Lanka as often as I wanted. Fortunately, my company was flexible enough to let me work remotely from Sri Lanka. So around late 2020, I packed up and moved back home.

And honestly? For a while, it was great. I was close to my family, close to everyone I cared about. Working remotely from Sri Lanka while being paid by a Singapore company was a pretty sweet setup. I had a comfortable workflow, the time zones worked out fine, and my wife also had a job. Life felt settled.

## Then things started falling apart

If you've been following what happened in Sri Lanka, you probably know where this is going. The economic crisis hit hard. The government really messed things up, and ordinary people had to deal with the consequences. We're talking about shortages of basic necessities - electricity, water, gas. Things most people take for granted just weren't available anymore.

During this period, I was in regular contact with friends who had already moved overseas. Most of them said the same thing - maybe it's time for you to think about moving too. And as a father, that really got me thinking. It wasn't just about me anymore. I had to consider what kind of future I could provide for my daughter. Could I guarantee her a stable, secure life if things kept going the way they were?

After a lot of thought, I decided it was time to look at options outside Sri Lanka.

## Finding the right path

There are many ways to move to another country, especially from a third world country like Sri Lanka. But I didn't want to take just any route. I wanted to find a path where I could put my skills to use - a proper skill migration.

My wife and I spent a lot of time researching different parts of the world. We looked at Europe, we looked at Australia. The US was never really on our list for multiple reasons, and honestly, they didn't have a clear path for skill migrants anyway.

We eventually landed on Australia. The skill migration pathway was well-defined, the process was transparent, and it felt like the right fit for our family.

## The grind

What followed was about two years of hard work. And when I say hard work, I don't mean coding or engineering work. I mean paperwork, documentation, background checks, and certificates. We had to do medical checkups - for me, for my wife, and for my daughter. And not just once. We had to redo them at different stages of the process.

If you've ever gone through an immigration process, you know how mentally exhausting it can be. There's always another document to gather, another form to fill, another step to complete. It feels like it never ends.

## The call

I still remember the day I got my state nomination. That was a huge milestone and I was over the moon.

But the moment that really sticks with me is when my migration agent called to tell me that our permanent residency had been approved. After two years of grinding through the process, it was finally done.

We were incredibly happy. But here's the thing - it was also bittersweet. We got what we wanted, but the reality of what it meant started sinking in. We were going to leave everything behind. Our parents, our immediate family, the house we had built, the life we had created.

My daughter had just made friends, she had her cousins around her. All of that was going to change.

I remember that day being quite overwhelming, trying to process all of those emotions at once - the excitement of a new chapter and the sadness of closing the current one.

## Looking back

When I think about this whole journey, it wasn't a single decision. It was a series of decisions, each one building on the previous. COVID pushed me to Sri Lanka. The economic crisis pushed me to think about leaving. Being a father pushed me to actually do something about it. And the skill migration pathway gave me a way to do it on my own terms.

I don't think there's ever a perfect time to make a move like this. You just reach a point where staying put feels riskier than starting over. That's where I was, and I'm grateful we took the leap.

More on how things went after the move in a future post 🤞.
